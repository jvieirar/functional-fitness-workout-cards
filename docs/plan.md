# Functional Full-Body Training Design

## Purpose

Create a sustainable home-first training system for a 35-year-old, 88 kg, experienced calisthenics trainee with strong upper-body capacity, recurrent low-back symptoms with no diagnosed hernia, and goals of whole-body strength, sports transfer, posture, trunk and glute strength, plyometrics, reaction speed, and calisthenics skill development. Fat loss is addressed by the separate nutrition and daily-activity plan, not promised as an outcome of this workout design.

The program must fit around a 9-to-5 job, preserve evenings for personal projects, take about 40 minutes per formal workout, accommodate Tuesday-night badminton plus a likely weekend badminton session, and work both with outdoor equipment and indoors while travelling or during bad weather.

This is general exercise programming, not diagnosis or rehabilitation. Existing physiotherapy advice takes precedence.

## Weekly Architecture

| Day | Morning | Evening |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Full-body A: front lever, upper-body strength, moderate lower-body strength; no plyometrics | Personal projects |
| Tuesday | Handstand + V-sit skill practice and mobility | Badminton |
| Wednesday | One short skill focus plus recovery mobility | Meal prep or occasional badminton |
| Thursday | Full-body B: pistol squat, optional lateral plyometrics, strength | Personal projects |
| Friday | Front lever or handstand/V-sit skill practice | Personal projects |
| Weekend day without badminton | Mobility plus a short pistol/weak-skill session; Saturday Full-body C only when there is no weekend badminton | — |
| Weekend badminton day | Mobility only before badminton | Badminton/recovery |

In a normal two-badminton week, Full-body A and B are the two formal strength sessions. Badminton plus skill mornings provide the remaining training frequency. A third full-body session (C) is used only in a week without weekend badminton, on Saturday. Sunday then remains a recovery day before Monday A.

If badminton occurs Tuesday and Wednesday, Thursday becomes mobility and easy skill work. Full-body B moves to Friday and C is omitted. There must be at least one non-strength day between formal full-body sessions. Do not schedule lower-body strength or plyometrics on the same day immediately before badminton.

Dedicated HIIT is omitted in two-badminton weeks. In a one-badminton week, one optional six-minute HIIT block may replace—not follow—the trunk/accessory finisher in Full-body B or C, provided the back and legs feel recovered.

## Session Timing

Formal full-body sessions target 36–42 minutes, normally close to 40:

1. Preparation and setup: 5 minutes
2. One calisthenics skill: 7 minutes
3. Low-volume plyometric work: 3 minutes on B only when recovery permits
4. Strength supersets: 20–22 minutes
5. Trunk/accessory finisher: 3–5 minutes
6. Transitions are included in these blocks

Skill-only mornings target 16–25 minutes: six to eight minutes of mobility, one primary skill for seven to ten minutes, and—only when time permits—one secondary skill for four to six minutes.

Minimum skill exposure in a normal two-badminton week:

- Monday: front lever during Full-body A
- Tuesday: handstand primary, V-sit secondary
- Wednesday: V-sit primary
- Thursday: pistol squat during Full-body B
- Friday: front lever primary, handstand secondary
- Weekend non-badminton morning: pistol squat

This gives each starting skill two planned weekly exposures. Saturday C in a no-weekend-badminton week adds a third exposure to the weakest skill.

## Readiness and Pain Rules

- Green: normal muscle effort or mild stiffness that improves during warm-up. Continue.
- Yellow: localized discomfort, reduced range, or symptoms that worsen set to set. Reduce range or load, choose a simpler variation, or perform mobility only. Indoor exercises are not automatically safer.
- Stop and arrange clinical assessment for new, changing, recurrent, or radiating symptoms before progressing loaded hinges, unilateral leg work, or plyometrics.
- Seek emergency assessment for saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change. Seek urgent clinical assessment for new or progressive leg weakness.
- Exercises should not cause symptoms that remain meaningfully worse the following day.
- Skill work, jumps, and loaded strength all stop before technique deteriorates.

## Daily Morning Mobility — 8 to 10 Minutes

Perform gently; this is preparation, not a flexibility test.

1. Crocodile or 90/90 breathing: 5 slow breaths
2. Cat-camel: 6 controlled repetitions, small comfortable range
3. Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch with glute squeeze: 30 seconds per side
4. Open-book thoracic rotation: 5 per side
5. Ankle rocks over toes: 8 per side
6. Deep squat hold with supported breathing: 30–45 seconds
7. Glute bridge: 8 controlled repetitions with a two-second hold
8. Scapular wall slides or band pull-aparts: 8–12 repetitions

On a back-sensitive morning, replace deep squat and bridge work with comfortable walking and the movements previously approved by the physiotherapist.

## Skill Progressions

All skill sets remain technically clean, with generous rest and no grinding.

### Front Lever — Monday and Friday; optional Saturday C

Outdoor/bar option:

1. Scapular pull-ups: 1 set of 5–8
2. Tuck front-lever hold: 3 sets of 6–12 seconds with 45–60 seconds rest
3. When 3 × 12 seconds is clean for two sessions: use advanced tuck, one-leg extension, or slow tuck-lever rows

Indoor/travel option without a rated bar:

1. Hollow-body hold or dead-bug press: 2 × 15–25 seconds
2. Banded straight-arm pulldown from a rated anchor: 2 × 8–12
3. Optional seated band row with deliberate scapular depression: 1 × 10–15

The indoor substitute builds prerequisites but is not equivalent to actual lever practice. A stable, correctly rated indoor pull-up station materially improves consistency.

### Handstand — Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday; optional Weekend

1. Wrist preparation: circles, palm pulses, and finger pulses for 60–90 seconds
2. Chest-to-wall handstand: 2 × 20–40 seconds
3. Wall toe-pulls or heel-pulls: 2 × 3–5 controlled attempts
4. Freestanding kick-ups: 3–6 quality attempts, stopping before fatigue

Use parallettes if wrists prefer neutral loading. When 2 × 30 seconds chest-to-wall plus five controlled toe-pulls are clean for two sessions, shift more practice to freestanding kick-ups. When three freestanding holds exceed five seconds in one session, progress to controlled entries and longer holds. Regress to shorter wall holds if line, wrist comfort, or shoulder elevation deteriorates.

### V-Sit — Tuesday secondary focus and Wednesday primary focus; optional Friday

1. Seated pike compression lifts: 2 × 6–10
2. Tuck L-sit or one-leg-extended L-sit on parallettes: 3 × 8–15 seconds
3. Alternating leg extensions from tuck support: 1–2 × 3–5 per side when time permits

When 3 × 15 seconds in tuck plus 2 × 10 clean compression lifts are achieved for two sessions, progress to one-leg extension. Then build a 3 × 10-second full L-sit before emphasizing hip elevation toward V-sit. Regress when the knees bend unintentionally, shoulders collapse, or back discomfort appears. Use a pain-free spinal and hip range; do not force compression through back pain.

### Pistol Squat — Thursday and the weekend non-badminton morning

1. Ankle and squat preparation: 1 minute
2. Controlled full pistol, counterweighted pistol, or assisted pistol: 2–3 × 3–5 per side
3. Use a three-second eccentric when full repetitions become inconsistent

Select the easiest variation that preserves a stable foot, controlled knee path, and level pelvis. Add backpack loading only after 2 × 5 clean repetitions per side for two sessions.

## Plyometric and Reaction Work

Keep contacts low because badminton already provides substantial jumping and reactive load.

Thursday only, unless Wednesday included badminton or badminton is scheduled later that day. Choose one jumping drill per session:

- Lateral bound and stick: 2 × 4 per side
- Or pogo hops: 2 × 8–10
- The following eligible session may substitute split-step to random left/right cue: 3 × 10 seconds

Start with 16–24 contacts. Add no more than four contacts after two symptom-free sessions with unchanged landing quality. Stop when landings become noisy, balance worsens, jump height drops, or the back becomes uncomfortable. Do not add jump volume merely to create fatigue.

## Full-Body A — Monday

Preparation and skills:

- Daily mobility shortened to 5 minutes
- Front-lever block: 7 minutes
- No plyometrics because Tuesday badminton follows

Strength Superset 1 — three rounds:

- Push: weighted backpack push-up, ring push-up, or deep-parallette push-up — 6–12
- Pull: strict pull-up/chin-up — 4–8; indoor alternative: strong band row — 10–15
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Strength Superset 2 — two rounds:

- Knee-dominant: rear-foot-elevated split squat or reverse lunge — 6–10 per side
- Hinge: single-leg Romanian deadlift with kettlebell, dumbbell, or backpack — 6–10 per side
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Trunk/accessory finisher — one or two rounds as time allows:

- Side plank, initially without hip dips — 20–35 seconds per side
- Bent-knee calf raise (soleus emphasis) — 10–15 per side

## Full-Body B — Thursday or Friday

Preparation and skills:

- Daily mobility shortened to 5 minutes
- Pistol-squat practice: 7 minutes
- Lateral plyometric/reaction block: 3 minutes, omitted after Wednesday badminton or before same-day badminton

Strength Superset 1 — three rounds:

- Vertical push: pike push-up, feet-elevated pike push-up, or wall handstand-push-up progression — 5–10
- Pull: ring row, cable row, one-arm kettlebell/dumbbell row, or heavy band row — 8–12
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Strength Superset 2 — two rounds:

- Squat: goblet squat with kettlebell/dumbbell or front-loaded backpack squat — 8–12
- Hip extension: weighted glute bridge, hip thrust from the floor, or hamstring walkout — 8–15
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Trunk/accessory finisher — one or two rounds as time allows:

- Pallof press from a rated anchor or dead bug with full exhale — 5–8 per side
- Short-lever Copenhagen plank — 15–25 seconds per side

## Full-Body C — Saturday in Weeks Without Weekend Badminton

Preparation and skills:

- Daily mobility shortened to 5 minutes
- Front-lever or weakest-skill practice: 7 minutes
- No mandatory jumps

Strength Superset 1 — three rounds:

- Push: ring dip progression, parallel-bar dip, close-grip push-up, or weighted floor push-up — 5–10
- Pull: chin-up/pull-up, ring row, cable pulldown, or heavy band pulldown/row — 5–12
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Strength Superset 2 — two rounds:

- Unilateral leg: step-up on a verified stable surface or reverse lunge — 6–10 per side
- Posterior chain: kettlebell Romanian deadlift, kickstand RDL, or hamstring walkout — 8–12
- Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises

Trunk finisher — two rounds:

- Bird dog with three-second reach — 5 per side
- Bear-plank shoulder tap or elevated shoulder tap — 6–10 per side

Optional HIIT in a one-badminton week only, replacing the trunk finisher:

- Six minutes alternating 30 seconds hard / 30 seconds easy
- Use shadow boxing, fast low-impact step patterns, or another back-comfortable mode
- Omit when sleep, back, knees, or badminton recovery is suboptimal

## Indoor and Outdoor Exercise Map

| Pattern | Outdoor/full equipment | Indoor/travel |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical pull | Pull-up, chin-up, tuck lever | Banded pulldown/straight-arm pulldown from a rated anchor; no-anchor rows are maintenance, not an equivalent substitute |
| Horizontal pull | Ring row, cable row | Band row, one-arm kettlebell or dumbbell row when available |
| Horizontal push | Ring or weighted push-up | Floor, backpack, or parallette push-up |
| Vertical push | Ring support/dip, pike push-up | Pike push-up, wall handstand progression |
| Squat/lunge | Goblet squat, split squat, pistol | Backpack/kettlebell squat, unilateral versions, pauses, slow eccentrics, or greater safe range |
| Hinge | Loaded RDL, single-leg RDL | Kettlebell/backpack hinge; use unilateral versions, pauses, slow eccentrics, or stronger/doubled bands for overload |
| Hip extension | Hip thrust, glute bridge | Floor bridge, hamstring walkout |
| Carry | Kettlebell/dumbbell carry | Suitcase march in place |
| Anti-rotation | Cable or band Pallof press | Band Pallof press if safely anchored; otherwise side plank |

The 15 kg kettlebell may be too light for bilateral leg or row work; use the progressively loaded backpack, stronger/doubled bands, unilateral variations, pauses, slower eccentrics, or greater safe range until heavier adjustable dumbbells are available. Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling. Band pulldowns and Pallof presses require a rated anchor. Pull-up stations must be rated for body weight plus added load and dynamic use.

## Grease-the-Groove Practice

Use two to four days per week. Select only one primary grease-the-groove movement per day and perform two to four mini-sets spread across the day. Keep at least five repetitions in reserve, or the equivalent margin for holds. Do not use a movement within roughly 24 hours before or after hard training of the same pattern.

Suggested starting doses:

- Pull-ups: 2 repetitions per mini-set when the bar is available
- Push-ups: 5–7 repetitions
- Handstand line hold: 15–25 seconds
- Band pull-aparts or scapular work: 8–12 repetitions
- Supported pistol pattern: 1–2 clean repetitions per side, no more than twice weekly

Do not use jumps, loaded pull-ups, ab-wheel repetitions, or fatiguing lower-body sets for grease-the-groove. GTG is the first training component removed when badminton performance, wrists, elbows, shoulders, legs, or back deteriorate.

## Progression

Strength work uses two or three repetitions in reserve. First build repetitions through the stated range. When every set reaches the top of its range with clean form for two appearances, progress one variable and return to the lower end of the range:

1. Add a small amount of load
2. Increase range of motion
3. Use a harder leverage or unilateral variation
Change only one variable at a time. Begin with two rounds of each strength superset for the first two weeks if returning from inconsistent training. Move the first superset to three rounds in weeks three and four. Every fifth week, or earlier if badminton performance, sleep, joints, or back symptoms decline, reduce strength sets and skill holds by roughly one-third.

Skill progression requires two consecutive sessions at the top quality target before advancing. Plyometrics progress through cleaner and more reactive contacts before adding volume.

## Assessment of the Current Circuit

The current circuit already has a good full-body foundation: horizontal push and pull, unilateral knee-dominant work, hinging, lateral trunk work, and posterior-chain activation.

Likely exercise names:

- Hollow body to elbows touching knees: hollow-body crunch, tuck-up, or jackknife variation
- Side plank with hip contact and opposite-elbow rotation: side-plank hip dip plus thread-the-needle
- Superman with hands moving backward: prone superman arm sweep
- “Romanian squats/splits”: likely Bulgarian or rear-foot-elevated split squats if the rear foot is supported

Recommended changes:

- Keep push-ups, rows, split squats, and single-leg RDLs.
- Add true vertical pulling whenever a safe bar is available and a vertical-push progression.
- Rotate trunk functions instead of performing repeated loaded spinal flexion, lateral movement/rotation, and extension in every round.
- Use dead bugs or hollow holds, side planks, Pallof presses, bird dogs, carries, and glute bridges as the primary trunk menu.
- Keep the dynamic crunch, side-plank rotation, and superman sweep only if comfortable, one at a time, and as accessories rather than the entire core strategy.
- An ab wheel is a later anti-extension progression; it should begin from a short range with no lumbar sag and should not be used during a back flare.

## Tracking

Record only:

- Exercise variation, load, sets, and repetitions or hold time
- Session effort from 1–10
- Back status before, during, and the following morning: green, yellow, or red
- Badminton session and unusual fatigue
- One monthly benchmark for each skill, without max-effort testing every week

The program succeeds when strength and skill quality trend upward, badminton remains energetic, back flare frequency does not increase, and the routine leaves enough energy for work and personal projects.
