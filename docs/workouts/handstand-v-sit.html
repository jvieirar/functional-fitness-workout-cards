TUE · WED · FRI SKILL MORNINGS

Handstand + V-sit Skills

Skill-only mornings use 6–8 minutes of mobility, one primary skill for 7–10 minutes, and a secondary skill for 4–6 minutes only when time permits.

16–25 MINPRIMARY SKILL FIRST · SECONDARY ONLY WHEN TIME PERMITS

Complete instructions

Skill-only mornings use 6–8 minutes of mobility, one primary skill for 7–10 minutes, and a secondary skill for 4–6 minutes only when time permits.

Weekly sequence

  • Tuesday: handstand primary and V-sit secondary.
  • Wednesday: V-sit primary.
  • Friday: front lever primary and handstand secondary.

Handstand practice

  • Wrist preparation: circles, palm pulses, and finger pulses for 60–90 seconds.
  • Chest-to-wall handstand — 2 × 20–40 seconds.
  • Wall toe-pulls or heel-pulls — 2 × 3–5 controlled attempts.
  • Freestanding kick-ups — 3–6 quality attempts, stopping before fatigue.
  • After 2 × 30 seconds chest-to-wall plus five controlled toe-pulls are clean for two sessions, shift more practice to kick-ups.
  • When three freestanding holds exceed five seconds in one session, progress to controlled entries and longer holds.
  • Regress to shorter wall holds if line, wrist comfort, or shoulder elevation deteriorates; use parallettes if wrists prefer neutral loading.

V-sit pathway

  • Seated pike compression lifts — 2 × 6–10 repetitions.
  • Tuck L-sit or one-leg-extended L-sit on parallettes — 3 × 8–15 seconds.
  • Alternating leg extensions from tuck support — 1–2 × 3–5 per side when time permits.
  • After 3 × 15 seconds in tuck plus 2 × 10 clean compression lifts for two sessions, progress to one-leg extension.
  • Build a 3 × 10-second full L-sit before emphasizing hip elevation toward V-sit.
  • Regress when knees bend unintentionally, shoulders collapse, or back discomfort appears. Use a pain-free spinal and hip range; do not force compression through back pain.

Progression

Build a clean full L-sit before emphasizing V-sit height; shift to entries after consistent wall-line control.

Card safety rule

Stop if symptoms worsen, radiate, or technique deteriorates. Follow physio guidance and read the full safety notes.

Safety notes

Stop and arrange clinical assessment for new, changing, recurrent, or radiating symptoms before progressing loaded hinges, unilateral leg work, or plyometrics. Exercises should not cause symptoms that remain meaningfully worse the following day. Skill work, jumps, and loaded strength all stop before technique deteriorates. Seek emergency assessment for saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change. Seek urgent clinical assessment for new or progressive leg weakness.

Readiness rules

  • Green: normal muscle effort or mild stiffness that improves during warm-up—continue.
  • Yellow: localized discomfort, reduced range, or symptoms worsening set to set—reduce range or load, choose a simpler variation, or perform mobility only.
  • Indoor exercises are not automatically safer. Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling.
