Grease-the-Groove Movement Snacks

Use grease the groove on 2–4 days/week. Choose one primary movement per day and perform 2–4 mini-sets spread across the day.

2–4 MINI-SETSKEEP AT LEAST 5 REPS IN RESERVE · NEVER CHASE FATIGUE

Complete instructions

Use grease the groove on 2–4 days/week. Choose one primary movement per day and perform 2–4 mini-sets spread across the day.

Rules

  • Keep at least five repetitions in reserve, or the equivalent margin for holds.
  • Do not use a movement within roughly 24 hours before or after hard training of the same pattern.
  • Mini-sets should stay fresh and technically clean rather than creating fatigue.

Suggested starting doses

  • Pull-ups — 2 repetitions per mini-set when the bar is available.
  • Push-ups — 5–7 repetitions.
  • Handstand line hold — 15–25 seconds.
  • Band pull-aparts or scapular work — 8–12 repetitions.
  • Supported pistol pattern — 1–2 clean repetitions per side, no more than twice weekly, using stable support.

Prohibited and removal rules

  • Do not use jumps, loaded pull-ups, ab-wheel repetitions, or fatiguing lower-body sets for grease the groove.
  • Remove GTG first when badminton performance, wrists, elbows, shoulders, legs, or back deteriorate.

Progression

GTG is the first training component removed when badminton performance, joints, legs, or back deteriorate.

Card safety rule

Stop if symptoms worsen, radiate, or technique deteriorates. Follow physio guidance and read the full safety notes.

Safety notes

Stop and arrange clinical assessment for new, changing, recurrent, or radiating symptoms before progressing loaded hinges, unilateral leg work, or plyometrics. Exercises should not cause symptoms that remain meaningfully worse the following day. Skill work, jumps, and loaded strength all stop before technique deteriorates. Seek emergency assessment for saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change. Seek urgent clinical assessment for new or progressive leg weakness.

Readiness rules

  • Green: normal muscle effort or mild stiffness that improves during warm-up—continue.
  • Yellow: localized discomfort, reduced range, or symptoms worsening set to set—reduce range or load, choose a simpler variation, or perform mobility only.
  • Indoor exercises are not automatically safer. Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling.
