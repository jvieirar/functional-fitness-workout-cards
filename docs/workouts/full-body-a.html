36–42 MINNO PLYOMETRICS — BADMINTON TUESDAY

Complete instructions

Monday's formal strength session combines front-lever practice, upper-body strength, and moderate lower-body work. No plyometrics because Tuesday badminton follows.

Preparation and skill — 12 minutes

  • Daily mobility shortened to 5 minutes.
  • Front lever block — 7 minutes.
  • Outdoor: scapular pull-ups 1 × 5–8; tuck front-lever hold 3 × 6–12 seconds with 45–60 seconds rest.
  • Indoor without a rated bar: hollow-body hold or dead-bug press 2 × 15–25 seconds; banded straight-arm pulldown from a rated anchor 2 × 8–12; optional seated band row 1 × 10–15.
  • Indoor prerequisite work is not equivalent front-lever exposure.

Strength superset 1 — 3 rounds

  • Weighted backpack, ring, or deep-parallette push-up — 6–12 repetitions.
  • Strict pull-up or chin-up — 4–8 repetitions; indoor alternative: strong band row — 10–15 repetitions.
  • Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises.

Strength superset 2 — 2 rounds

  • Rear-foot-elevated split squat or reverse lunge — 6–10 repetitions per side.
  • Single-leg Romanian deadlift with kettlebell, dumbbell, or backpack — 6–10 repetitions per side.
  • Rest 45–75 seconds after both exercises.

Trunk and accessory finisher

  • One or two rounds as time allows.
  • Side plank, initially without hip dips — 20–35 seconds per side.
  • Bent-knee calf raise with soleus emphasis — 10–15 repetitions per side.

Progression

Keep 2–3 reps in reserve. Reach the top of the range twice, then add load or use a harder variation.

Card safety rule

Stop if symptoms worsen, radiate, or technique deteriorates. Follow physio guidance and read the full safety notes.

Safety notes

Stop and arrange clinical assessment for new, changing, recurrent, or radiating symptoms before progressing loaded hinges, unilateral leg work, or plyometrics. Exercises should not cause symptoms that remain meaningfully worse the following day. Skill work, jumps, and loaded strength all stop before technique deteriorates. Seek emergency assessment for saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change. Seek urgent clinical assessment for new or progressive leg weakness.

Readiness rules

  • Green: normal muscle effort or mild stiffness that improves during warm-up—continue.
  • Yellow: localized discomfort, reduced range, or symptoms worsening set to set—reduce range or load, choose a simpler variation, or perform mobility only.
  • Indoor exercises are not automatically safer. Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling.
