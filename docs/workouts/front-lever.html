Front Lever Skills

Monday provides formal lever practice and Friday is the primary skill morning; Saturday C can add an optional exposure. Actual lever work requires a stable rated bar.

7–10 MINACTUAL LEVER WORK REQUIRES A STABLE RATED BAR

Complete instructions

Monday provides formal lever practice and Friday is the primary skill morning; Saturday C can add an optional exposure. Actual lever work requires a stable rated bar.

Outdoor or rated-bar option

  • Scapular pull-ups — 1 set of 5–8 repetitions.
  • Tuck front-lever hold — 3 sets of 6–12 seconds with 45–60 seconds rest.
  • When 3 × 12 seconds is clean for two sessions, use advanced tuck, one-leg extension, or slow tuck-lever rows.
  • The pull-up station must be stable and rated for body weight plus added load and dynamic use.

Indoor or travel prerequisite work

  • Without a rated bar: hollow-body hold or dead-bug press — 2 × 15–25 seconds.
  • Banded straight-arm pulldown from a rated anchor — 2 × 8–12 repetitions.
  • Optional seated band row with deliberate scapular depression — 1 × 10–15 repetitions.
  • A stable, correctly rated indoor pull-up station materially improves consistency.

Equivalence and safety

  • No-anchor rows and hollow work maintain prerequisites but are not equivalent front-lever exposure.
  • Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling.
  • Stop before technique deteriorates; no grinding.

Progression

Advance one leverage step only after the 3 × 12-second target is clean for two sessions.

Card safety rule

Stop if symptoms worsen, radiate, or technique deteriorates. Follow physio guidance and read the full safety notes.

Safety notes

Stop and arrange clinical assessment for new, changing, recurrent, or radiating symptoms before progressing loaded hinges, unilateral leg work, or plyometrics. Exercises should not cause symptoms that remain meaningfully worse the following day. Skill work, jumps, and loaded strength all stop before technique deteriorates. Seek emergency assessment for saddle numbness or bowel/bladder change. Seek urgent clinical assessment for new or progressive leg weakness.

Readiness rules

  • Green: normal muscle effort or mild stiffness that improves during warm-up—continue.
  • Yellow: localized discomfort, reduced range, or symptoms worsening set to set—reduce range or load, choose a simpler variation, or perform mobility only.
  • Indoor exercises are not automatically safer. Never use an improvised door, furniture edge, or unstable anchor for high-tension pulling.
